# Case 292: source-grounded audit

The question is `not innovative(aliyah)`. Gold is `B`; the saved Prolog
certificate is `contradicted`; P0, P1 and P2 all answered `C`.

The certificate is source-grounded. From the English text:

1. `trusts instinct` and the exclusive alternative `thinks logically or trusts
   instinct, but not both` exclude `thinks logically`.
2. Together with `does not evaluate data effectively`, this excludes `makes
   accurate conclusions`, whose consequence requires exactly one of those two.
3. `does not have attention to detail` then excludes `precise in measurements`.
4. `meets deadlines` plus `does not work independently` forces collaboration;
   collaboration gives communication; communication is mutually exclusive with
   reliable findings. Thus reliable findings are excluded.
5. If Aliyah were accurate, she would be precise or reliable. Both alternatives
   are excluded, so accurate is excluded.
6. The source says `accurate or innovative`; with accurate excluded, innovative
   follows. Therefore `not innovative` is false: answer `B`.

This is one discriminating certificate opportunity in the six-case pilot. Four
other certificates were `unknown`, and case 404 was `invalid_program`. The pilot
shows zero P1-to-P2 label changes, not that certificates were ignored: P2 reasons
explicitly cite model evidence on cases 128, 300 and 336. It does not establish
general runtime ineffectiveness.
